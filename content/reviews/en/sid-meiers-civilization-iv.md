# Sid Meier's Civilization IV — IGN Review

- Source: [IGN](https://www.ign.com/articles/2005/12/04/sid-meiers-civilization-iv-review)
- Author: Robert 'Apache' Howarth
- Published: 2005-12-04

Sid Meier is a name that needs no introduction. Walk up to a stranger, ask if they know who Sid Meir is (pronounced my-her). Odds are, if they can't give you a definitive "yes", they'll at least agree that they've heard the name before. How do you explain this phenomenon? Sid has been around for so long and produced so many great games that he's an honest to goodness legend. One of Sid's many popular franchises is Civilization (or Civ, for short) and the fourth iteration of series is what we'll be looking at today.

 What is Civ? Simply put, it's a turn-based Civilization simulator. Starting out with a stone age settler, you build your first community, research new technologies, expand, plan infrastructures, militaries, foster  relationships with outer cultures you meet along the way, and eventually either gets along with them and win diplomatically, or for the more bloodthirsty, militarily.  Phew. That was a mouthful. The game can be as simple or complex as you want: Workers can be automated, there are advisors to streamline things, and in the custom options you can toggle any number of features to tailor the game to your liking. Enjoy prosperity and peace? Play without wars. Would you rather build massive military empires and crush every other culture on the planet? You can do that too. The level of customization is impressive to say the least.

  (Click to Enlarge!)

  The biggest changes in Civ IV over earlier editions are the audio and visual upgrades. The game now uses a real 3D engine and audio in general is wonderful. Did I mention Spock does the technology voice overs? Oh yeah, it's all good. Besides improving the window dressing they added a ton of new units, technology and systems to manage your empire, and also made some of the not-so addictive qualities of the game less tedious such has population and social issues.

 Visually -- considering what type of game it is -- the new 3D graphics in Civ IV look great. It's a really cool change to see everything in 3D, and the little animations are a nice upgrade too. You can zoom into ground level and oversee the goings on with an over the shoulder view, or zoom out literally into orbit and play that way. Each major zoom layer has its own audio; ground level you hear the bustle of whatever activity is happening, while higher up you're treated to multi cultural classic music

   The only real problem I faced is that after a couple centuries of playing, the game gets laggy. I'm not sure if it's a memory leak or there's just so much going on that it kicks the CPU's ass, but after around 1900 the game starts to chug like a smoker jogging up a couple dozen flights of stairs. Other than that, the game is what you make of it. The amount of options you can tweak in a custom game is staggering, so if you like something and want more of it, bam - you're set. If you hate some particular quality, you can change that too.

 As far as an organized single player experience goes, there are a bunch of pre-made scenarios like the 13 Colonies vs the British Empire. But, the meat and potatoes of the game come from random scenarios. You can control empires ranging from the American to the Spanish and every possible culture you can imagine in-between. A normal game will span from the stone age to distant future space age and there's a number of ways to win a match. In a time limited scenario, the Civ with the most points in the year 2050 wins. Conquest victory is for players who take everyone else out on the map. Cultural victory takes place when someone builds three legendary cultural cities, plus there's diplomatic victory for the player who builds the UN first and passes a certain number of resolutions. The coolest however was the space race victory. The first Nation who launches a ship to Alpha Centauri wins. Each ending has its own movie as well.

Civ IV, simply put, rocks the ages.

 The Good: Addictive gameplay; thousands of hours of fun; as complex or simple as you like it; they got Spock in the back!  The Bad: Performance gets chuggy near the end
