# Mario & Luigi: Bowser's Inside Story AU Review — IGN Review

- Source: [ign](https://www.ign.com/articles/2009/10/21/mario-luigi-bowsers-inside-story-au-review)
- Author: Cam Shea
- Published: 2009-10-21
- Score: 8.4

Let's open the review with a few of the things we've learned about Bowser during the course of Bowser's Inside Story. 1) Hot drumsticks will replenish his health. 2) Put him on a treadmill and he'll run so fast he sets himself on fire. 3) He can change his shell, according to stat bonuses/the vicissitudes of fashion. 4) He likes having Princess Peach inside him.

 After all these years, we're still discovering vital statistics about good ol' Bowser. In fact, that's one of the things that is so great about the Mario RPG and Mario & Luigi games – you get the inside scoop on Nintendo's characters. Literally in this game's case. These games revel in revealing something close to the reality of their lives – in a tongue in cheek, whimsical way, of course. It's through these games that we've found out more about Bowser's love for Peach, for instance, while Luigi's timid nature and (lack of) fame gets far more time in the spotlight – in Superstar Saga no one knew who he was, while Mushroom Kingdom denizens recognised Mario by his trademark jump alone.

 Each game in the Mario & Luigi series has its own unique hook. The first game – Mario & Luigi: Superstar Saga on GBA, was an innovative RPG in that it had the player controlling both characters at once, with all sorts of team-up moves that allowed for traversal puzzles in the world and interesting special moves in battles. The second game, Mario & Luigi: Partners in Time, doubled the fun, pairing up Mario & Luigi with their baby selves, which introduced a little more complexity into both puzzles - the pairs would often split to separate screens, for puzzles that spanned both - and battles. It was, however, very much an extension of the mechanics of the first game.

 And so it is again, but this time around, the hook is that Mario and Luigi spend a fair portion of the game inside Bowser. Yes, the intrepid duo – along with half the Mushroom Kingdom – are sucked into Bowser's body as the result of a plan hatched by the villain of the piece, Fawful. You may remember him as the zany sidekick from Superstar Saga. Well, he's back, and so is his unique cadence, which gets a little tiresome this time around. Ah well.

 In any case, the gameplay is a split between controlling Bowser as he roams the Kingdom in the series' familiar three quarter viewpoint, and between Mario and Luigi's adventures in his colon. (Well, actually you'll go to places like 'Funny Bone', 'Flab Zone' and 'Rump Command'.) Bowser has his own set of stats which can be levelled up, his own gear to equip and his own moves, and really feels powerful from the get-go. In the world he can dash-punch to plough through stone obstructions and get across gaps, and breath fire to torch trees and palms to clear the way forward and reveal secrets.

 In battle he's a mass of muscle, with a powerful punch (with great wind-up animation), a flame attack, and the ability to call on various minions for his special moves. Ever wanted to set a parade of Goombas on fire? Now you can. Ever wanted to see Bowser shot out of a slingshot at his enemies? Again, now you can. He can also use his Vacuum move (the source of all this trouble) to suck certain things off and out of enemies. Certain Goombas have lollipops, for instance, that can boost health, while there are 15 'Blitties' (block kitties) hidden inside enemies to find. Most significantly, however, lesser enemies can be sucked into Bowser wholesale, with the viewpoint zooming in on his stomach, where Mario and Luigi must then dispatch them.

 The battle mechanics for the Moustachioed Two are reminiscent of the last couple of games. You know, the usual stuff – jump attacks, hammer attacks and specials like taking it in turns to kick a shell at an enemy. There are, of course, new additions, such as the King-Size Luigi move pictured above, or the bizarre special where Luigi dons some kind of spring helmet and Mario must repeatedly bounce off his head to gain height before he comes crashing down on the enemy. This move, appropriately enough, is called 'Jump Helmet'. Just how they come up with these zany names we'll never know.

 Truth be told, it all feels a little too familiar for those of us who've spent significant time with the last couple of games, but it's still compelling, and the reliance on Action Commands (timed button presses during combat) means you're never just watching battles play out – you're always boosting your attacking power and attempting to avoid taking damage. Once again, enemies all have a 'tell', so by observing them you'll be able to work out what attack they're about to launch and counter it. There's a decent variety of foes, but they're not hugely inspired, and they're not very hard. In fact, the entire game is far too easy. Whether that's a problem or not, of course, will come down to the individual.

 Like Partners in Time, Bowser's Inside Story also takes advantage of puzzles that are solved across both screens. In one section (below left) you can make Bowser drink from a fountain, which floods the area Mario and Luigi are in, raising certain blocks which are hampering their progress and allowing them to swim about. By turning Bowser away from the fountain, the area drains, and you need to utilise both states to progress. You can also do cool stuff like drain an area to dry out a jelly fish – all the easier to beat it in battle. At other times Bowser needs a little help shifting something. You know, castles and things. So you'll have to head to the 'Arm Center' and boost his muscles via a rhythm action-y mini-game. Or you might have to help Bowser digest his food. No, really.

 You can switch between Bowser and Mario/Luigi at any time, complete with a shift of the music – same melody, but different instruments and tone. Mario and Luigi's adventures inside the beast are presented from side-on, and play out a bit like a platformer, with familiar mechanics returning – Luigi can bonk Mario on the head with a hammer so he's small enough to fit through small holes, while they can also team up to do a Spin Jump to get across gaps that are too wide to jump.

 To be honest though, we were expecting a little more invention and creativity in the design of the areas inside Bowser. Wouldn't this be a great excuse to go wild? To push the series' visual design and conceptual design? Sadly, it's not the case. This series, despite its charms, has settled into a comfortable pattern.

 In fact, Bowser's Inside Story feels a little uninspired at times – particularly as far as the characters go. The series has always utilised quirky, oddball characters to help give it flavour, and this game is no exception... well, except that they mostly fall flat. Note to Alphadream – a bad French accent does not a character make. (Although we do like hearing Bowser called 'Monsieur Turtle Bits'.) Nor do wacky speech patterns. (Oh Fawful, you really should have stayed a sidekick.) Or the incessant use of the word 'globin'. (Yeah, we get it, they're 'emoglobins' inside Bowser – can we move on already?)

 All that said, the game is certainly not without personality and charm. It's hard not to love Bowser, for instance – he's a great inclusion, and suffused with his own brash style. And we love that he doesn't head bonk save squares and blocks – he punches the hell out of them. There are also numerous small touches that add character, like the various hilarious dances Luigi does on the battle screen. If you can watch Luigi swinging his hips from side to side while rhythmically pointing at the battle selections above him without at least cracking a smile, well, you're a miserable bastard. We kid, we kid. Kinda.

### Verdict

So where does this all leave us? Bowser’s Inside Story is undoubtedly a very good game, and if you’ve never played this series before it’s worth snapping up immediately. For the veterans, however, it’s a touch too familiar, and - once again - just far too easy. There’s an awful lot of hand holding in this game, and enemies will rarely – if ever – present much of a threat. We could forgive the familiarity if it was challenging, and on the flip side, if it was too easy but wildly inventive, that would be okay, but the team really hasn’t broken the mould here.


It is still engaging, however, as the core design is excellent. And it’s a fairly length adventure too. Hell, you don’t even find out that there’s a badge system in the game until seven or eight hours in… around the time that it’s finally revealed that the main story has something to do with Stars (which is a legal requirement for all the RPG titles bearing Mario’s name). It’s a long road to earn all the special moves, work your way up through the levels, get to all the equipable gear and try out all the badges too. It’s just that – despite the changes – it feels a bit like we’ve done this all twice before.


As you can imagine, this is a tricky game to score. We really enjoyed it, but it’s hard not to feel that Alphadream should have tried a little harder to shake things up. The following is in no way intended as a diss, but if you’re after the gaming equivalent of a security blanket – warm, comforting and easy, this game is a great way to pass the time. If you want something that’s moving the medium forward, look elsewhere.
