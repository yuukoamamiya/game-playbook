# Pushmo Review — ign Review

- Source: [ign](https://www.ign.com/articles/2011/12/08/pushmo-review)
- Author: Lucas M. Thomas
- Published: 2011-12-08
- Score: 9.5

It took until nearly the end of its first calendar year, but Nintendo's 3DS seems to finally be firing on all cylinders. The release of Super Mario 3D Land just a few weeks ago gave the system the killer app it needed on the physical media front. Then the new firmware update released earlier this week brought the on-board software itself up to speed, finally activating long-promised built-in features like 3D video recording.

And today? The trifecta's complete -- because in addition to offering true winners in physical packaged games and on-board applications, Nintendo's at last brought us an exclusive download-only eShop title that is an absolute must-have game. Ladies and gentlemen, I give you Pushmo .

Developed by Intelligent Systems -- the same brilliant minds most commonly known for their work on the Advance Wars, Fire Emblem, Paper Mario and WarioWare franchises -- Pushmo is a beautifully original, absolutely charming and oftentimes devious little portable puzzler. The game casts you as Mallo, a happy, rotund hero who's tasked with rescuing children who've become trapped inside some unique pieces of playground equipment -- the Pushmo. Each Pushmo is a stack of colorful blocks, and Mallo must push, pull and leap across the tops of each individual block in each puzzle to be able to reach the spot at the top where a boxed-in child is waiting to be saved.

It's instantly obvious that this game was developed specifically for the 3DS, as each Pushmo is presented in a way that maximizes the system's stereoscopic visual effect. Mallo approaches each stack of blocks at the beginning of each level, and they're all initially flattened back against the background. He then grabs hold of a block and pulls it forward -- into the foreground one step -- and it, of course, simultaneously gets "closer" to your face. He can then pull it again to a second, further distance and one last time to a third, furthest-out and closest-to-you distance.

It's incredible. It really shouldn't seem so impressive, since these aren't flashy explosions or wild enemy characters leaping out at you -- they're just boxes. But these boxes look brilliant in their stereoscopic simplicity. The game's wonderfully vibrant color palette has probably got something to do with that, too, since this game world looks like the stuff a Crayola factory worker's dreams are made of.

In typical Nintendo fashion, the earliest levels teach you the mechanics of how all of this pushing and pulling works. A wisened old Pushmo builder, Papa Blox, teaches you the techniques you'll need to succeed -- though there aren't many to master. The basic idea is that you need to grab hold of blocks and pull them out in an order that builds a basic staircase for you to ascend to the top. Sometimes you'll need to grab a block from the side to pull it instead, or reposition yourself to be able to pull a block out further without falling off the side.

But pulling, pushing and jumping are all that you've really got to work with -- and it's incredible how that limited array of maneuvers can form the foundation for hundreds of different brainteasers. Even the simplest-looking stack of blocks can become a true head-scratcher, and it feels truly satisfying to finally figure out and clear a level that's had you stumped. (Also in typical Nintendo fashion, though, you're never left stuck on any stage. You're given the option to skip any that are too tough for you and come back to them later.)

Pushmo's main campaign of over 250 puzzles is topped off by a few extra gameplay features, like a time-rewinding power that lets you undo the past several seconds' worth of moves like in Catrap, Braid or Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time. You also get a reset switch on every stage to just erase all your progress and start back from scratch if you want, and later levels mix up the basic concept of building blocky staircases by introducing ladders and tubes -- which work a bit like Mario's warp pipes, forcing you to reach different spots on the stack of blocks, jump inside and pop up somewhere else on the structure.

All of those extra elements add convenience and extend the time it'll take you to finish Pushmo's default set of challenges, but then the truly winning extra feature asserts itself -- the Pushmo Studio. Unlocked as soon as you clear the game's first set of tutorial levels, the Studio is where you can create your own levels. Then, even better, you can generate QR Codes of your creations to be able to share them with friends across the room or across the Internet.

I can tell this is going to get addicting, and no doubt Pushmo communities are already forming in Internet forums to exchange everyone's personal best brainteasers and/or re-created NES sprites. (That's a bonus, too, as these blocky puzzlers naturally lend themselves to Nintendo nostalgia -- and the company must have recognized that, because you'll run across a familiar Mario face or two on your way through Mallo's quest.)

Are you sold on Pushmo yet? You should be -- it's brilliant. But though the game is this good, I do find myself with one minor complaint. Pushmo isn't up for any 'Passing.

It would have been going the extra mile for Nintendo to include StreetPass or SpotPass support in Pushmo, but sadly neither made the cut. StreetPass functionality could have let me share my favorite created puzzle with strangers I tag wirelessly, or SpotPass could have automatically downloaded a new level made by Nintendo's own designers (or selected from community submissions) every day. The lack of those features is unfortunate -- but it's a minor complaint, really, and the reliance on QR Codes should ensure that the level-sharing process feels more personal in the long run.

Yes, it's taken the 3DS a long time to find its footing. The 3DS eShop? Even longer. But the arrival of Pushmo demands that all owners of Nintendo's newest handheld stand up and pay attention to the company's newest download shop, as it's an absolute must-have game. The first-party killer app the eShop has needed is here -- and beyond even the 3DS, this may very well be the best downloadable game Nintendo's ever offered in any format.
